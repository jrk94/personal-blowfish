Leadership programs are a tightrope. They always live on the edge of being buzzwordy marketing tools. But is this an original sin, or a misimplementation?

## Organization

One of the key questions any organizations must face is, why do they exist? Why are they any different than any other organization.

Are we the most innovative? Are we the one's that care the most about our customer base? Are we the one's with the more grit? Are we the cheapest alternative?

Throughout time, companies can even remake themselves searching for optimizing their ability to create value. A company that doesn't know itself is doomed to a kafkaesque abyss.

Living in a disjointed chaos where the individuals that compose the organization are endlessly making contradictory and erratic decisions.

## Leaders

Leaders have an important roll in every organization. They help the company build a structured vision throughout it's layers.

Sometimes the vision and strategy of a company is seen as a monolithical top down dictate. Nothing can be furthest from the truth. 

Any one that has ever worked in any organization knows that when people want to blown smoke screens and boycott a strategy, it is very hard for upper management to effictively act. 

It is also true that a global vision is concerned about global value creation and is not really concerned with local synergies.

Leaders have a pivotal role in enforcing the global strategy and searching for local value creation. The local value creation is often were exponential value comes from.

Leaders throughout the organization have an important place in suggesting improvements to the global strategy, see gaps in the adherence of the strategy to reality and even providing ideas to improve that vision.

## What is a Leader?

This is the most important part to any effective leadership program. It is often quite hard to understand what is a leader. As a leader is highly coupled with the organization where he is leading and the perception of the organization of itself.

A very assertive and aggressive person, may be a valuable leader for a sales oriented company, but may be seen as rash and risky in a banking company.

It is also very difficult to project effective leaders that already exist in the organization on to emergent leaders. Every organization has leaders, the question is if they are good or bad leaders. An engineering focused organization tends to promote leaders that are very analytical and rational but maybe less socially apt. Does this mean that if your organization has a lot of soccially inept leaders, that a leadership program for scouting emergent leaders, should focus only on those who are technically gifted?

This is very hard to answer it has a lot to do with the global company strategy and not that focused on the leaders you already have their profile but about the profiles that you want to cultivate inside the organization.

Imagine a scenario where you have an organization that originally was based on a industrial setting. Where everything was highly stractified, but know the organization wants to start shifting to a services delivery approach. Does it make sense to keep training the leaders with the same profile?

## Nature vs Nurture

Does your organization organically create leaders? 

A leadership program may be pointless, if you already have a synergy in place where your organization already creates leaders. Sure, they can have more training about x, y or z, but a leadership program is not about that.

A leadership program is fast track bootcamp for organizations that are suffering from a middle layer gap of leadership. This often seen in companies that are struggling to modernize and implement change, or companies that are in hypergrowth. If as C level executive you are not surrounded by people with a high level of critical thinking coupled with a high capacity to execute, you are sure to not be able to implement any of your great ideas.

One of the major decisions to invest in leadership building is what I call deck shuffling. Sometimes, you get dealt a bad hand of cards. A set of great people in the wrong seats or with a negative perspective regarding the company vision, or even a set of bad people. It's is normal for everyone to be dealt a normal distribution of talent, but remember too many incompetent people around you, will create a strong synergy of bad decisions. The upside is that investing in quality people with the right mindset and training has a very strong global impact. 

The question remains, if you go to the deck will you have aces or deuces.

The goal of an organization is having a strong deck, full of people with a healthy mix of high talent and high skill.

## DNA of a Modern Leader

I will layout what is my generic view on what is a leader. But remember this has to be adjusted to your organization needs and global strategy.





The DNA of a Modern Leader
In today’s fast-evolving world, where industries are disrupted by startups, digital platforms rewrite business models, and entire workforces go hybrid overnight, the definition of a leader is being reimagined.

Gone are the days when leadership was synonymous with seniority or hierarchy. The modern leader isn’t just the loudest voice in the room or the person with the corner office. Instead, they’re often the quiet architect behind the scenes—cultivating culture, challenging assumptions, and shaping the future through purposeful action.

So what exactly makes up the DNA of a modern leader?

1. Vision, Grounded in Reality
A modern leader is a visionary—but not the dreamy, detached type. They see the long-term horizon and chart a path toward it, but they do so with a firm understanding of current capabilities, constraints, and context. Their vision is actionable, not aspirational poetry.

2. Resilience Over Rigidity
The best leaders today aren’t inflexible pillars; they’re resilient bridges. They adapt without compromising their values. They welcome challenge and ambiguity, seeing it not as chaos, but as opportunity. Grit is no longer just a personal trait—it's an organizational requirement.

3. Builders of People and Culture
Leadership today is less about commanding and more about cultivating. Modern leaders recognize that their role is not just to deliver results, but to build environments where others thrive. They know that psychological safety, diverse thinking, and shared ownership lead to sustained innovation.

4. Powered by Curiosity and Candor
True leaders ask more questions than they answer. They’re curious learners and candid communicators. They’re not afraid to admit what they don’t know or to change direction when new information comes to light. Their honesty isn’t just refreshing—it’s strategic.

5. Deeply Purpose-Driven
Today’s leaders understand the "why" behind what they do. Whether it’s building better products, enabling sustainable industries, or creating equitable workspaces, they connect their team’s daily efforts to a larger mission. That clarity of purpose creates resilience and commitment in others.

Final Thought
Leadership today isn’t defined by position—it’s defined by impact. The DNA of a modern leader includes a blend of humility and drive, vision and execution, logic and empathy. These leaders aren't waiting for change—they're leading it.

If you're building a company, a team, or even just your own career, now’s the time to rethink what leadership looks like. Because the future won’t be shaped by the loudest or the longest-tenured—it will be shaped by those who build it intentionally.

## What is TypeDoc?

At its core, **TypeDoc is a documentation generator for TypeScript projects**. It takes your TypeScript source code, leverages its rich type information, and parses your JSDoc-style (or [TSDoc](https://tsdoc.org/)-compliant) comments to create beautiful, browsable HTML API documentation. Think of it as your project's personal archivist, meticulously cataloging every class, interface, function, and variable.

## Why TypeDoc?

1. **TypeScript-Native Understanding**: Unlike generic documentation tools, TypeDoc is built from the ground up for TypeScript. It inherently understands the nuances of TypeScript's type system – interfaces, generics, union types, intersection types, access modifiers, and more. This means it accurately represents your API's structure without requiring verbose, redundant comments.

2. **Leverages Your Existing Comment**s: TypeDoc embraces the well-established JSDoc syntax (and the more modern TSDoc specification). If you're already documenting your functions and classes with JSDoc comments, TypeDoc will readily consume them, enriching the generated output. This means less new syntax to learn and more leverage from your existing work.

3. **Rich, Navigable Output**: The generated documentation is typically in a clean, HTML format, complete with:

- Search functionality: Quickly find what you're looking for.
- Hierarchical navigation: Easily explore modules, classes, and members.
- Syntax highlighting: Code examples are readable and clear.
- Cross-referencing: Links to related types and members within your documentation.

4. **Customization**: TypeDoc offers various themes and extensive configuration options, allowing you to tailor the look and feel of your documentation to match your project's branding or integrate seamlessly into a larger documentation website.

---

## How TypeDoc Works (The Simplified Flow)

1. **Installation**: You install TypeDoc as a development dependency in your project.

2. **Configuration**: You tell TypeDoc where your source files are (src/index.ts for example) and where you want the output (--out docs).

3. **Comment Parsing**: TypeDoc scans your TypeScript files for declarations (classes, functions, etc.) and their associated JSDoc/TSDoc comments.

4. **Type Information Integration**: It analyzes the TypeScript types (e.g., string, MyInterface, Array<T>) and incorporates this crucial information into the documentation.

5. **HTML Generation**: TypeDoc renders all this information into a set of interlinked HTML files (and supporting CSS/JS), typically with an index.html as the entry point.

## Getting Started with TypeDoc

Let's look at a quick example to see how simple it is:

1. Install TypeDoc:

```Bash
npm install typedoc --save-dev
```

2. Add JSDoc/TSDoc Comments to Your Code:

```TypeScript

 * specifically for oven telemetry. It processes readings and setpoints from
 * oven zones and dispatches them to a telemetry system.
 *
 *
 * This task is activated by its `activate` input. Upon activation, it iterates
 * through provided oven data, constructs telemetry payloads, and posts them
 * using `SystemCalls.postTelemetry`. It handles success and error conditions,
 * emitting appropriate output signals.
 *
 * It's designed to work with structured oven data, matching readings with
 * corresponding setpoints and enriching the data with relevant tags and ISA95
 * hierarchy information.
 *

 * * `activate`: `any` - Triggers the execution of the task. Set to any value to activate.

    /**
     * Initializes the task.
     *
     * @remarks
     * Registers any required handlers and ensures the settings are sanitized
     * against default values before execution begins.
     */

    public override async onInit(): Promise<void> { ...
```

3. Add a Script to package.json:

```JSON
{
  "name": "@criticalmanufacturing/connect-iot-controller-engine-custom-dataplatform-tasks",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  ...
  "scripts": {
    ...
    "doc:build": "npx typedoc --entryPointStrategy Expand src",
    "doc:start": "open-cli docs/index.html",
    "doc:build:start": "npm run doc:build && npm run doc:start"
  },
  "devDependencies": {
    "typedoc": "^0.25.0",
    "open-cli": "^8.0.0" // optional
  }
}
```

4. Generate Your Docs:

```Bash
npm run doc:build
```

After running this command, you'll find a docs/ folder in your project root, containing the generated HTML documentation. Open docs/index.html in your browser, you can also run, if you install `open-cli`:

```Bash
npm run doc:start
```
